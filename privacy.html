IRIS

Your eye stays on your phone.

IRIS turns a photo of your eye into a picture of your iris. All of that happens on your device.

What happens to your scan

  • The camera feed and your photos are processed in your browser, on your phone. They are not uploaded, stored on a server, or seen by anyone.
  • Finding your iris and drawing the final picture both run on the device (a small model and a renderer shipped with the site).
  • Nothing is kept after you close the page, except in the two cases below.

Kept on your phone only

  • If you buy the HD pack or order a print, your current scan is saved in your browser's storage on this phone, so it's still there when you come back from the payment page. It never leaves the phone.
  • If you bought the HD pack, a note that it's unlocked is saved in your browser.
  • You can remove both by clearing this site's data in your browser settings.

When something is sent

  • Print and gift orders. When you order, the finished picture (not your original photo) is uploaded so it can be printed, and kept only as long as needed to make and ship the order.
  • Payments are handled by Stripe. Stripe receives your payment details, email and, for orders, your shipping address. We don't see your card. See Stripe's privacy policy.
  • Printing and shipping are done by a print-on-demand partner, who receives the picture and your shipping address.
  • Sharing is up to you: pictures and videos go only where you send them with your phone's share sheet.

Hosting and ads

  • The site is hosted on Vercel, which keeps standard technical logs (like IP addresses) to run the service.
  • If ads are shown, they come from Google AdSense, only after the scan, never to HD buyers. Google may use cookies; you'll be asked for consent first. See how Google uses data for ads.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask what we hold about you, and ask us to delete it (for example a print file). Since scans aren't uploaded, there's usually nothing to delete. Contact us: github.com/chaoui-ahmed/iris.

Iris images can count as biometric data. That's why IRIS is built to process them on your phone and not on a server.
